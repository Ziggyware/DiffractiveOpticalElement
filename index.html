physics, model & simplifications (read me)

Model. Scalar thin-element diffraction, Fraunhofer (far-field) limit. The mask is a N×N phase-only relief; the replay is its 2-D discrete Fourier transform computed with a Stockham radix-2 FFT in fragment shaders (log₂N passes per axis, float32 ping-pong, or float16 “reduced precision”). A unit-modulus mask radiates Σ|F|² = 1 (Parseval), so intensities are absolute, not arbitrary.

Sampling. Bin spacing in direction-sine space is λ/(N·p), and the zero order sits at the centre of the natural-order FFT. Everything the replay panel shows is sampled from that grid, so the replay scale is proportional to λ: the same mask is transformed three times (640 / 532 / 450 nm) and composited additively on one screen plane. The cutoff is sin θ ≤ λ/2p, which in bin units is exactly the inscribed circle of the FFT — drawn as a dotted ring.

Etch depth. The etch is fixed, so the phase at wavelength λ is φ_λ = φ_design·(λ_design/λ). Drag λ off-design: the three replays slide apart, the blaze loses to the zero order and the conjugate twin brightens.

Zero order & twin image. Both are real physics and are never hidden: the zero order is the un-diffracted spot at the centre (the crosshair), and the point-mirrored conjugate image appears whenever the mask phase is symmetric in intensity (e.g. anything binary, like a 2-level hologram). They are left in as motifs. The FFT is a real transform: no Hermitian padding, no “remove the twin” tricks.

Validity. Scalar thin-element theory degrades in the resonance regime p ≲ 2λ — the badge turns amber there. Below p ~ λ the element is a metasurface and this model is decoration, not prediction.

Simplifications, stated

  • No material dispersion. The substrate index is a constant n = 1.5; the only λ-dependence is the λ_design/λ phase scaling. No Sellmeier data, no absorption, no Fresnel reflection losses.
  • Scalar, thin, paraxial-adjacent. Polarisation, vector/rigorous coupled-wave effects, finite etch-sidewall slope, and near-field propagation are ignored. The element is a pure phase screen at z=0+.
  • Fraunhofer only. The replay is the far field. A real DOE at finite distance shows Fresnel ripple; that is not modelled.
  • No apodisation, no aperture obliquity factor. The exit pupil is a hard-edged square (so the point spread function has that faint 4-fold sinc carpet, which is physical for a square aperture but is not a real mount).
  • Quantisation is exact, and it bites. Etch levels are perfect equal-height steps. The blaze is sampled 8 times per period, so an even level count (4, 6, 8) balances the staircase and cancels its zero order exactly, while 5 or 7 levels leave an uneven staircase with a real 0.6-1.6% zero-order residue in the replay. No depth errors, line-edge roughness or sidewall angle.
  • Gerchberg–Saxton runs on a reduced grid (N/2, max 512) with complex-exponential upsampling, a quadratic-phase init (to spread the spectrum and cut speckle) and a warm start from the previous frame, 6–12 iterations per frame. It optimises the quantised phase, so the RMS readout is honest. The target carries a non-zero background level — with a perfectly black background the phase-only problem collapses to the trivial field ≡ 0 solution.
  • Exposure is a log tone map normalised by an on-GPU energy reduction (RMS speckle amplitude). It is a display choice; the underlying intensities stay absolute.
  • Animated quantities (zone-lens focus, rotozoom, l drift, audio drive) move the mask, not the light. Under calm or prefers-reduced-motion every animation is frozen and no luminance modulation above 3 Hz is produced.
